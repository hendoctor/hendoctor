## Hey, I'm James.

I lead engineering and enterprise operations at **Centralized Supply Chain Services**, where we build technology behind a multi-billion-dollar restaurant supply-chain ecosystem.

My work spans applications, architecture, data, cloud infrastructure, DevOps, and governed AI. I care as much about growing engineers, establishing clear ownership, and choosing the right problems as I do about the code.

I also founded **[RootedAI](https://rootedai.tech)** because I think most companies underestimate what small teams with talent can build, operate, secure, scale, and maintain with AI embedded throughout the development lifecycle.

I play with code. I lead engineers, builders, creatives, and business executives. I design and architect systems. I build software to fix things that annoy me.

Sometimes those solutions become companies or products.

## Recent work

### ⚙️ CodeCare

Developing an AI-assisted workflow for ongoing code maintenance: investigate an issue, make a focused improvement, and prepare a reviewable pull request with a clear summary of changes, validation, and remaining risks.

### 🪺 Nestwise

Building product intelligence for families, with product search, barcode lookup, and personalized AI explanations as the focus. I'm working on making product and ingredient information easier to understand, with clear sources and thoughtful handling of personal context.

### 🏗️ Enterprise platforms

At CSCS, I work on supply-chain applications, partner experiences, and data platforms. The goal is to connect people, information, and decisions while giving the team clear ownership from implementation through ongoing operation.

### 🌱 RootedAI

Helping organizations identify worthwhile opportunities for AI, build the applications and integrations around them, and support adoption and ongoing improvement. Strategy, engineering, and the people using the system all belong in the same conversation.

[Explore our work →](https://rootedai.tech/work)

### 🧠 Reusable AI skills

Turning recurring prompts, learning methods, and engineering workflows into reusable skills. I'm exploring how persistent context, useful tools, and clear permissions help agents take on more work with results people can inspect.

## Things I believe

### Leadership & ownership

**Leaders should still understand the code.**

**What’s possible keeps expanding. Knowing what’s worth building matters most.**

**Good leadership gives people context, clear ownership, and room to make decisions.**

**A small team that owns the problem end to end can move fast.**

**You don't need more meetings when the system can provide the context.**

**Humans should spend their time on judgment, creativity, relationships, and consequential decisions.**

### Building useful products

**The best tool is usually the one nobody needs training to use.**

**If the application doesn't offer API/MCP support, I'm not using downloading it.**

**Shipping teaches you things architecture diagrams can't.**

**The fastest way to understand a problem is often to build something small and put it in front of the people who live with it.**

**A spreadsheet is often a product requirement pretending not to be one.**

**Abundant possibilities gives us more room to explore before we commit to a direction.**

**The best technology strategy usually removes more than it adds.**

**The most valuable systems connect decisions to actions, not dashboards to more dashboards.**

**Good software makes complicated operations feel boring.**

### AI & automation

**The best automation handles the routine and escalates the judgment calls.**

**Small teams with the right AI leverage and context can outperform much larger organizations.**

**AI-native engineering is bigger than writing code faster. It changes how software gets planned, built, tested, reviewed, secured, and operated.**

**AI agents need boundaries, tools, feedback, and accountability, not just better prompts and context.**

**The next user type of your software will be an agent. Design the interfaces accordingly and as necessary.**

**Don't automate a bad process until you've asked whether the process should exist at all.**

**The real promise of AI isn't cheaper software. It's making previously uneconomical ideas worth building.**

**The future org isn't humans versus AI. It's humans designing increasingly capable systems of humans and agents.**

### Architecture & operations

**Developer experience is an architecture problem.**

**Security belongs in the design, not in the remediation backlog.**

**Automation without observability is just a faster way to create invisible problems.**

**A feature isn't done when it works. It's done when someone can operate, observe, recover, and maintain it.**

And lastly:

> If I have to do something annoying three times, there's a decent chance I'm already building software for it.
